$30 trillion in motion.

The next great wealth transfer isn't about houses. It's about where $30 trillion in concentrated equities moves — and what platforms win by being accessible first.1

Sep 2026 — Educational Analysis

Where wealth sits today

Of the roughly 5.7 billion adults globally, only 1.5% hold net assets above USD 1 million, yet this thin slice — some 57.5 million people — controls a disproportionate and growing share of global wealth.1

Source: UBS Global Wealth Report 2026.

The age-wealth gap (1983–2022)

Every age group below 75 has lost ground relative to the national average household net worth over the past 39 years — while households 75 and older have gained sharply.2

Portfolio mismatch: where the money actually is

The 75+ cohort holds 31.2% of assets in stocks with minimal debt. Under-35 households hold 46.5% in housing with 65.4% debt-to-net-worth ratio.2

The transfer problem

What's leaving: Concentrated equities, low-cost basis, debt-free (75+ cohort)

Where it's going: Housing-debt-heavy portfolios with minimal stock exposure (under-35 cohort)

The opportunity: Inheritors will liquidate and reallocate. Fast.

The market they're entering (dangerously concentrated)

The S&P 500 top 10 now account for 40.7% of the index — more than double the 18–23% average from 1990–2015.3

Bitcoin: The alternative gaining traction

Bitcoin shows 6.21% average monthly returns but with extreme volatility (19.71% standard deviation), making it a high-risk, high-return asset.4 By contrast, traditional equities (S&P 500) average 1.08% monthly returns with 5.38% volatility. Younger inheritors exploring alternative stores of value face a structural shift in platform accessibility.

The capital flows next

① Equities will accelerate (but with limits)

$30T in inherited stock hits a market already at record concentration. Heirs facing capital gains tax will seek diversification.1

↓

② Alternative markets emerge (Kalshi, prediction markets)

Younger inheritors have zero institutional friction with platforms like Kalshi. Easier onboarding, real-time execution, narrative-driven. Firms that lower the barrier win massive AUM.

↓

③ Crypto takes a piece (if regulation permits)

Bitcoin's 6.21% monthly returns attract speculative capital, but extreme volatility keeps it material rather than dominant.4 A generation raised on apps will explore it as a portfolio diversifier.

↓

④ Housing/physical assets lose relative appeal

Younger cohorts are already housing-poor and debt-heavy. Liquid, tradeable assets win. Real estate becomes a lagging asset class for this cohort.

What firms need to win

🎯 Accessibility

Kalshi-style simplicity. One-click entry. Minimal friction.

📊 Real-time execution

Not quarterly reviews. Inheritors reallocate at speed. Live trading, instant settlement.

🔗 Integration

Connect inheritance flows to first move. API-first UX.

The AUM prize

Capture even 2% of reallocating wealth = $600B+ in new AUM within a decade. Firms that can't offer accessibility (traditional advisors) lose that flow.

Housing gets left behind

Projected asset allocation shift as wealth transfers and younger cohorts gain control.

The housing squeeze

Millennial + Gen-Z inheritors aren't buying second homes with their windfall. They're reallocating to liquid markets where returns are faster and entry is easier. Real estate faces structural headwinds as capital pulls away.
